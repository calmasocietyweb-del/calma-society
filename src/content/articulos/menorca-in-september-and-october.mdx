There is a moment, around the second week of September, when Menorca lowers its voice. The cove car parks stop filling by mid-morning, the terraces recover their empty chairs, and the sea — always the last thing to cool down — stays warm as if summer had not checked the calendar. That is when what we call **the quiet season** begins: two months, September and October, in which the island offers nearly everything summer does, with a fraction of the people.

This is our deep reading of that window. If what you want is the whole year at a glance — month by month, season by season — it lives in our guide on **[when to visit Menorca](/en/article/when-to-visit-menorca/)**; here we linger where that guide moves on: what changes exactly from one week to the next, what stays open, what is celebrated, and how to make the most of the finest light of the year.

## The essentials, at a glance

- **The sea:** September is still **full swimming season**, with the water around **24-26 °C**; in early October some still swim, above all in the south.
- **The festivals:** the **Festes de Gràcia in Maó (7-9 September)** see the summer off in the capital; the equestrian cycle closes later in the month in Es Mercadal and Alaior.
- **The trail:** September and October are among the **best months of the year for the [Camí de Cavalls](/en/article/the-cami-de-cavalls/)** (the GR-223, some 185 km in 20 stages).
- **The calendar:** local-produce fairs, film and music festivals and a dense sporting programme; all of it in the island's **[what's on guide](/en/whats-on/)**.
- **The prices:** notably better value than July and August, without giving up the good weather.
- **Worth knowing:** seasonal coastal services **wind down** as autumn advances; confirm opening times before you travel.

## What is Menorca like in September?

September is, quite simply, **summer without the crowds**. The water sits at its warmest of the year — the 24-26 °C of full swimming season — and the southern coves get back the breathing room August takes from them. It is no coincidence that when we ask our own place guides for each cove's best season, September comes up again and again: it is the month we recommend for **[Macarella](/en/place/cala-macarella-en/)**, for **[Cala en Turqueta](/en/place/cala-turqueta-en/)**, for Cala Mitjana and for **[Pregonda](/en/place/cala-pregonda-en/)**, in the wild north. The local rule of reading the wind and choosing your coast — with the tramuntana, the south stays sheltered; without it, the north lies calm — works exactly as in summer, only now with a choice of parking spaces.

And then there is the farewell. The **[Festes de Gràcia](/en/whats-on/festes-gracia-en/)** fill Maó from 7 to 9 September with their *qualcada*, in a more serene register than high August: these are the festivals that see the summer off in the capital. The patron-saint cycle itself closes a week later with the **Festes de Sant Nicolau in Es Mercadal** (12-13 September); stay until the end of the month and there is still a coda: the **Festes des Caixers in Alaior** (26-27 September), the last equestrian date in the Menorcan calendar. All their dates are in our [what's on guide](/en/whats-on/).

## What is Menorca like in October?

October is another register altogether, and perhaps the most underrated of the year. The Menorcan autumn begins **mild and luminous**, growing more humid as the month advances; the light turns low and golden, the countryside greens again and the whole island seems to walk at a different pace. In our year-round guide we call it "a golden gift", and we stand by that: it is the month for those who come to **walk, look and eat**, rather than to lie on the sand.

And the swimming? No longer guaranteed, but not ruled out either: in the first days of October some still swim, especially in the more sheltered southern coves. Honesty demands the other half too: October is also the month when **seasonal Menorca retreats**. Beach bars, part of the coastal hospitality and some services close for the year; the towns keep living — that is precisely their charm — but it pays to **confirm what is open** before booking a table or an outing.

## What's on? The quiet season's calendar

Against the cliché that "nothing happens after August", the real calendar of these two months is surprisingly full. Everything below is verified in our **[what's on guide](/en/whats-on/)**, each entry with its official source:

**In September.** After the Festes de Gràcia (7-9), the **Menorca International Film Festival, FICME** (8-13), fills the island with screenings. The **[Cranc Illa de Menorca Festival](/en/whats-on/cranc-illa-de-menorca-festival-en/)** celebrates its tenth anniversary in Maó (17-19): an indie, electronica and pop festival that defines itself as "small, sustainable and right by the stage" — hard to imagine a better fit for this island. The following weekend, the **Fira Arrels** gathers Menorca's zero-kilometre produce in Es Mercadal (20-21). And the month closes on a sporting note: on 27 September the **Artiem Half Menorca** triathlon in Fornells coincides with the **Swim in Menorca** open-water crossing, 10 km from Macarella to Son Xoriguer past the loveliest coves of the south.

**In October.** The **[Menorca Doc Fest](/en/whats-on/menorca-doc-fest-en/)** (9-12) brings documentary film and photography with its Premi Biosfera; that same weekend, the **European Heritage Days** (9-11) open monuments and archaeological sites free of charge, **[Talayotic Menorca](/en/article/talayotic-menorca/)** included. The **Vedella Vermella food weeks** (9-18) devote two weekends to the native breed in restaurants across the island, and on the third weekend comes the **[Mahón-Menorca Cheese Fair](/en/whats-on/fira-formatge-mahon-menorca-mao-en/)** in Maó (16-18, pending confirmation in the official programme). The close is sporting again: the international **cyclotourism Volta** (23-25) and, on the 24th, the **[October Trail](/en/whats-on/october-trail-menorca-en/)** along the Camí de Cavalls, from Son Bou to Cala en Bosc.

## Unhurried coves and the Camí de Cavalls in its best light

If September belongs to the sea, October belongs to the trail. The **[Camí de Cavalls](/en/article/the-cami-de-cavalls/)** — the GR-223, some 185 kilometres circling the island in 20 stages — finds in September and October, together with spring, its finest conditions: gentle temperatures, clean light and few people. Long walking is a pleasure in the cool of autumn and gruelling in high summer; the quiet season solves that equation on its own.

Our suggestion is to combine the two: in September, a short stretch at dusk with a swim at the end; in October, whole stages without a glance at the clock. And for choosing a cove with judgement — which one, in which wind, with what access — our **[guide to the best calas](/en/article/best-calas-menorca/)** holds exactly as true outside August; only now you can skip the early alarm.

## The sunsets, with the island almost to yourself

There is one more argument, of the kind brochures never mention: the sunset. May, June, September and October offer **splendid sunsets with a fraction of the crowds** of high summer. The classic stages of the west — **[Punta Nati](/en/place/faro-de-punta-nati-en/)**, the Cap d'Artrutx lighthouse — can then be enjoyed as they deserve: in silence, with room to spare and no queue of cars. Our guide to **[Menorca's sunsets](/en/article/menorca-sunsets/)** walks through every viewpoint; in these months, almost any of them is a good idea.

## The autumn table: cheese, vedella and the island's produce

The quiet season is also the tastiest one. It is not just that tables free up: the island's food calendar concentrates precisely here. September's Fira Arrels and October's cheese fair put **[Mahón-Menorca DOP cheese](/en/article/mahon-menorca-cheese/)** and zero-kilometre produce centre stage; the vedella vermella weeks do the same for the native breed bound to the landscape of the Biosphere Reserve. Add the **[island's wine](/en/article/menorca-wine/)** and you have the Menorcan larder gathered into its most generous stretch of the calendar.

## Our reading

If we had to sum up the quiet season in two sentences, they would be these. **September is the summer August promises and does not always deliver**: the same sea, the same coves, the last horse festival — and room for everything. **October is the island for those who truly want it**: the noblest light of the year, the trail at its best, the local-produce fairs and the first deep calm of autumn, with the fair trade-off that part of the coast shuts for the year.

It is not the season for anyone who needs a guaranteed daily swim or the buzz of July. It is the season for those who understand luxury the way we do: space, time, and an island that becomes yours again.
