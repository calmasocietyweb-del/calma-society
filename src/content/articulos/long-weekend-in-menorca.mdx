Three days won't show you all of Menorca. They'll give you something better: the chance to see it properly. A long weekend — a bank holiday, a break stolen from the calendar — is plenty of time to understand what this island is about, provided you accept the only rule that matters here: choose little, and live it slowly.

Geography is on your side. Menorca is small: [Mahon](/en/article/mahon-natural-harbour) to [Ciutadella](/en/article/ciutadella-city-of-palaces) takes about 45 minutes on the Me-1, the road that runs the length of the island. Nothing is ever far — but that hides the classic short-trip trap: crossing the island twice a day to tick everything off. This itinerary does the opposite. One area per day, gaps left unplanned, and the conviction that in Menorca, luxury means not being in a hurry.

## The essentials

- **The scale works for you:** Mahon–Ciutadella, ~45 minutes on the Me-1; the airport is ~12 minutes from Mahon and ~45 from Ciutadella.
- **One area per day:** the east and Mahon's harbour, the west with a southern cove, and the north around Fornells. No needless crossings.
- **Southern coves in summer:** cars are **banned at Macarella from 1 June to 30 September** (shuttle from Ciutadella, taxi, bike or on foot); Turqueta and Mitjana keep car access plus a short walk, with car parks that fill first thing.
- **The wind decides:** when the tramuntana blows hard, swap the north day for the more sheltered south.
- **One base:** sleeping all three nights in the same place saves the hours that matter most here. If your base is Ciutadella, allow for the island crossing (~45 min) on departure day.

## How this itinerary is built

Each day has one centre of gravity — a town, a cove, a village — and room around it for the unplanned, which in Menorca tends to be the best part. The driving times we quote are the island's real ones, not brochure optimism. And there's an honest plan B for when the weather turns, because it does.

## Day 1 — Arrive, Mahon and its harbour

There is no better introduction to Menorca than its capital. The airport is about 12 minutes from **[Mahon](/en/article/mahon-natural-harbour)**: by mid-morning you can be walking the old town, leaning over the viewpoints above one of the largest natural harbours in the Mediterranean. The city is best explored without a map or a goal: Carrer Isabel II, the market, the [British legacy](/en/article/british-heritage-mahon) that shows in sash windows and wine-coloured façades.

The afternoon calls for the harbourside promenade, the water always at your side. For dinner, a charming option is nearby **[Es Castell and its Cales Fonts](/en/article/es-castell-the-quiet-harbour)**, the little harbour next door where the tables lean out over the water and the first evening of the trip ends the way it should: with no clock.

It's a deliberately gentle day. Arrive, get your bearings, change pace. Everything else can wait until tomorrow.

## Day 2 — Ciutadella and a southern cove

Today you cross the island — once, and with a reward. The Me-1 takes you from Mahon to **[Ciutadella](/en/article/ciutadella-city-of-palaces)** in about 45 minutes, and right by the road waits a stop worth making: the **Naveta des Tudons**, Menorca's most celebrated [Talayotic](/en/article/talayotic-menorca) funerary monument, with signposted parking and a short visit (check opening times before you go).

Ciutadella deserves the cool of the morning: the old harbour, the cathedral, the palaces and the lanes of golden stone. Then, the swim. The **[southern coves](/en/article/best-calas-menorca)** are the island's postcard, and with a single day it pays to choose just one, and choose it wisely:

- **[Macarella and Macarelleta](/en/article/macarella-macarelleta-coves)**, the most photographed. In summer (1 June–30 September) you **cannot drive there**: shuttle bus from Ciutadella, authorised taxi, bike, or on foot from Cala Galdana along the Camí de Cavalls.
- **[Cala en Turqueta](/en/article/cala-turqueta-cove)**, about 20 minutes' drive from Ciutadella plus a 10–15 minute walk through pines. Its free car park fills very early in high summer: in August, arriving around 8 am makes all the difference.
- **[Cala Mitjana](/en/article/cala-mitjana-cove)**, next to Cala Galdana: a car park and an easy 1.2 km path. In high season, before 10 am.

Whichever you choose, the formula is the same: go early or late, bring water and shade — none of the three has permanent services — and stay until the sun drops. The natural close to the day is the **[west-coast sunset](/en/article/menorca-sunsets)**, the island's most celebrated, followed by a quiet dinner in Ciutadella's harbour.

## Day 3 — The north, Fornells and the flight home

The last day changes landscape and mood. The north coast is the other Menorca: dark rock, wind, silence. From Ciutadella you reach **[Fornells](/en/article/fornells-fishing-village)** in about 33 minutes (around 20 if your base is Mahon), and the fishing village is the best possible argument for a slow morning: white houses, a sheltered bay, and the **[lobster stew](/en/article/fornells-lobster-stew)** that made its name, served facing the harbour and in no hurry at all.

A quarter of an hour away is **Cap de Cavalleria**, the island's northernmost point, with its lighthouse and a horizon you won't forget. And if time and energy allow, the **[northern coves](/en/article/northern-coves)** offer their wildest side: reddish-sanded Pregonda is earned on foot, a walk of about 30 minutes from the Binimel·là car park.

<Figure src="/uploads/fornells.webp" alt="The white-housed fishing village of Fornells, in its sheltered bay on the north coast of Menorca." caption="Fornells: the serene farewell a good trip deserves." credit="Adobe Stock" lang="en" />

For the journey back, plain numbers: from the Fornells area to the airport is a little over half an hour; from Ciutadella, about 45 minutes across the island. Leave a margin, and let the goodbye not be a race.

**A variant if your base is Mahon:** instead of returning to the central north, give the morning to the **[s'Albufera des Grau natural park](/en/article/s-albufera-des-grau)** — about 15 minutes from Mahon, flat walks and birdlife — and to the **Favàritx** lighthouse and its lunar landscape, half an hour from the capital. A summer caveat: car access to the lighthouse is restricted from 1 June to 31 October (in 2026 the closure was brought forward to mid-May) and you go up on the seasonal line 43 bus from Mahon; check the current year's calendar.

## If the weather turns

In Menorca the wind is not a footnote: it writes the script. When the **tramuntana** blows hard, the north loses its charm and the south sits sheltered; when the wind comes from the south, it flips. Before choosing the day's cove, check [where the sea is calm](/en/where-the-sea-is-calm) — the local habit that turns an average day into a good one.

And if it rains — rarely, but it happens — the trip doesn't fall apart: the two towns are at their best with wet streets and no crowds, and [Talayotic Menorca](/en/article/talayotic-menorca), a World Heritage Site, waits with 4,000 years of patience.

## Our take

A long weekend in Menorca works when it resembles the island: a few things, well chosen, at their own pace. Three centres of gravity — Mahon's harbour, a southern cove, the bay of Fornells — and air around them. Giving things up is the price, and also the secret: the Menorca you remember is not the one ticked off a list, but the one watched without hurry from a table by the water.
